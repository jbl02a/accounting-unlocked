# Curriculum

Eleven levels planned; six built. `LEVEL_META` in `src/components/LevelCard.jsx` is the
single source of truth for titles, eras and ordering — keep this file in step with it.

## Phase 1 · Contact (Period 1, 1491–1607)

**Level 1 · Before Contact (1491)** — Native North America by region: Pueblo
irrigation, Chinook fishing villages, Plains hunting, Iroquois confederation,
Mississippian Cahokia. The organising idea is that environment shaped social
organisation. Drills: region card sort, 3 questions.
Traps taught: no single "Native American culture"; horses are post-contact.

**Level 2 · Contact & the Columbian Exchange (1492–1607)** — what crossed in each
direction, the demographic collapse, and the causal chain from sugar to the Atlantic
slave trade. Drills: direction card sort, chronology, 4 questions.

**Level 3 · Three Empires (1500s–1600s)** — Spain, France and England compared by what
each wanted from the land, plus the Dutch. Drills: empire card sort, 4 questions.

## Phase 2 · The colonies (Period 2, 1607–1754)

**Level 4 · The Chesapeake (1607–1700)** — tobacco → labour hunger → land hunger →
conflict → slavery in law. Drills: chronology, Bacon's Rebellion cause/effect sort,
5 questions.

**Level 5 · New England (1620–1700)** — motive, covenant, town meeting, schools,
dissent, and war over land. Drills: New England vs Chesapeake sort, 5 questions.

**Level 6 · Middle Colonies & Lower South (1650–1750)** — grain and toleration against
rice and a Barbadian slave code; closes with all four regions in one table. Drills:
region sort, 5 questions.

### Planned, not yet built

**Level 7 · Slavery in British North America (1619–1750)** — the Middle Passage, the
difference between chattel slavery and indentured servitude, regional variation (gang
vs task), and resistance from Stono to everyday refusal. Needs care: resistance is the
part usually left out.

**Level 8 · The Atlantic World (1650–1754)** — mercantilism, the Navigation Acts, the
triangular trades, and salutary neglect as the habit that made 1763 feel like a
violation of rights already held.

**Level 9 · Colonial Minds (1700–1754)** — the First Great Awakening, the
Enlightenment, the Zenger trial, and how self-government became an expectation.

**Level 10 · Colonial Capstone (1491–1754)** — compare the four regions, then build a
thesis. The first level that asks for argument rather than recognition.

**Level 11 · Sourcing a Document (skill)** — HIPP, drilled against sources the student
has already met, so the skill is the only new thing.

## After Period 2

Units 3–9, in exam order of usefulness rather than strict chronology, plus DBQ and LEQ
practice with rubric-based feedback. See `course-alignment.md` for what is deliberately
deferred and why.
