# Curriculum

Twelve levels planned; eight built. `LEVEL_META` in `src/components/LevelCard.jsx` is the
single source of truth for titles, eras and ordering — keep this file in step with it.

## Phase 1 · Contact (Period 1, 1491–1607)

**Level 1 · Before Contact (1491)** — Native North America by region: Pueblo
irrigation, Chinook fishing villages, Plains hunting, Iroquois confederation,
Mississippian Cahokia. The organising idea is that environment shaped social
organisation. Drills: region card sort, 3 questions.
Traps taught: no single "Native American culture"; horses are post-contact.

**Level 2 · Contact & the Columbian Exchange (1492–1607)** — what crossed in each
direction, the demographic collapse, and the causal chain from sugar to the Atlantic
slave trade. Drills: direction card sort, chronology, 4 questions.

**Level 3 · Three Empires (1500s–1600s)** — Spain, France and England compared by what
each wanted from the land, plus the Dutch. Drills: empire card sort, 4 questions.

## Phase 2 · The colonies (Period 2, 1607–1754)

**Level 4 · The Chesapeake (1607–1700)** — tobacco → labour hunger → land hunger →
conflict → slavery in law. Drills: chronology, Bacon's Rebellion cause/effect sort,
5 questions.

**Level 5 · New England (1620–1700)** — motive, covenant, town meeting, schools,
dissent, and war over land. Drills: New England vs Chesapeake sort, 5 questions.

**Level 6 · Middle Colonies & Lower South (1650–1750)** — grain and toleration against
rice and a Barbadian slave code; closes with all four regions in one table. Drills:
region sort, 5 questions.

## Phase 3 · Empire and war (1650–1763)

Built ahead of the rest of Period 2 because the next class test is on the French and
Indian War and salutary neglect.

**Level 7 · The Atlantic World (1650–1754)** — mercantilism, the Navigation Acts, the
triangular trades, the Dominion of New England as a rehearsal for 1763, and salutary
neglect as the habit that made enforcement feel like tyranny. Drills: a "good for the
colonies / bad for the colonies" sort that deliberately has real entries in both
columns, then 6 questions.

**Level 8 · The French & Indian War (1754–1763)** — the two names for one war, the Ohio
valley, the Albany Plan, Native nations acting on their own interests, Britain's
expensive victory, the Treaty of Paris, Pontiac's War and the Proclamation Line, and
the debt that ends neglect. Drills: chronology from Fort Necessity to the Stamp Act,
a cause/result sort, then 8 questions.

The organising claim of both levels: **Britain won the war, and the victory is what
broke the relationship** — debt, threat removed, enforcement.

### Planned, not yet built

**Level 9 · Slavery in British North America (1619–1750)** — the Middle Passage, chattel
slavery against indentured servitude, regional variation (gang vs task), and resistance
from Stono to everyday refusal. Needs care: resistance is the part usually left out.

**Level 10 · Colonial Minds (1700–1754)** — the First Great Awakening, the
Enlightenment, the Zenger trial, and how self-government became an expectation.

**Level 11 · Colonial Capstone (1491–1763)** — compare the four regions, then build a
thesis. The first level that asks for argument rather than recognition.

**Level 12 · Sourcing a Document (skill)** — HIPP, drilled against sources the student
has already met, so the skill is the only new thing.

## After this

The rest of the imperial crisis (1765–1775), then Units 4–9, in exam order of
usefulness rather than strict chronology, plus DBQ and LEQ practice with rubric-based
feedback. See `course-alignment.md` for what is deliberately
deferred and why.
